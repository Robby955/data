Let $\mathscr U$ be local base of open neighborhoods of a point $x\in X$ with $\mathscr U$ totally ordered by inclusion.
Let $\mathscr A$ be a countable local pi-base for $x$.
Wlog assume $x$ is not an isolated point in $X$.
So given $A\in\mathscr A$, there is some $a\in A\setminus\{x\}$.
By {P2} there is some $U_A\in\mathscr U$ that avoids the point $a$; hence $A\not\subseteq U_A$.

*Claim*: the countable collection of $U_A$ for $A\in\mathscr A$ is a local base at $x$ (i.e., it is cofinal in $\mathscr U$ with respect to reverse inclusion).
Indeed, any $U\in\mathscr U$ contains some $A\in\mathscr A$.
And since $A\not\subseteq U_A$, we cannot have $U\subseteq U_A$.
Therefore, since $\mathscr U$ is totally ordered by inclusion, necessarily $U_A\subseteq U$.
